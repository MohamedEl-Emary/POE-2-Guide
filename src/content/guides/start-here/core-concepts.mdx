These are the five systems that every build in Path of Exile 2 is built from. You don't need to master them before you start, but knowing they exist will save you a lot of confusion.

## 1. Skill gems and support gems

Your skills don't come from your class. They come from <GlossaryTerm id="skill-gem">skill gems</GlossaryTerm>.

- **Uncut gems**, like <Item name="Uncut Skill Gem" /> and <Item name="Uncut Support Gem" />, drop as you play. When you use one, you choose which skill it becomes. See <GlossaryTerm id="uncut-gem" />.
- **Support gems** slot into a skill to change it: more projectiles, extra damage, a different element, and so on. Each support gem can only be used once across all your skills, so you have to decide where each one does the most good. Browse every [skill gem](/items/skill-gems/), [spirit gem](/items/spirit-gems/) and [support gem](/items/support-gems/).
- **Gem level** is one of the biggest sources of damage. Higher-level gems need a higher character level, so your damage naturally grows as you level up.

<Callout type="tip">
  Whenever you find a higher-level uncut gem, check whether it can upgrade your main skill. It's often the biggest damage increase available.
</Callout>

## 2. Spirit

Some skills stay switched on permanently: auras, buffs and <GlossaryTerm id="minion">minions</GlossaryTerm>. Instead of costing mana each time, they **reserve** <GlossaryTerm id="spirit" />.

You get Spirit mainly from specific campaign rewards and from some gear. Because it's limited, choosing what to spend it on is part of your build.

## 3. The passive tree

The <GlossaryTerm id="passive-tree" /> is a huge web shared by every class. Each class starts in a different place, close to the stats that suit it. You earn a point most times you level up, plus extra points from some quests.

Plan a path toward the clusters that help your main skill, and pick up life or energy shield along the way. A build guide's tree is the easiest way to start.

<Callout type="beginner">
  Made a mistake? You can refund passive points for <GlossaryTerm id="gold" />. Small fixes are cheap. See <GlossaryTerm id="respec" />.
</Callout>

## 4. Resistances

<GlossaryTerm id="resistances">Resistances</GlossaryTerm> reduce fire, cold, lightning and chaos damage. Elemental resistances normally cap at 75%.

Here's the catch: **each Act of the campaign permanently lowers your elemental resistances by 10%**. By the endgame you're at −60% before gear. If you don't pick up resistance on rings, belts, boots and other gear, enemies will suddenly start killing you very quickly.

Check your character sheet after every Act and aim to keep fire, cold and lightning at 75%.

## 5. Defences

Damage keeps you progressing, but defences keep you alive. The main layers are:

| Defence | What it does | Who uses it |
|---|---|---|
| <GlossaryTerm id="life" /> | Your health pool | Almost everyone |
| <GlossaryTerm id="armour" /> | Reduces physical hits | Strength classes |
| <GlossaryTerm id="evasion" /> | Chance for attacks to miss | Dexterity classes |
| <GlossaryTerm id="energy-shield" /> | Recharging layer over your life | Intelligence classes |
| <GlossaryTerm id="block" /> | Chance to stop a hit entirely | Shield users |

And the most important defence of all is free: the <GlossaryTerm id="dodge-roll" />. Rolling away from a boss's big attack beats any amount of armour.

## Putting it together

A healthy character has a strong main skill with good supports, enough life or energy shield for its level, capped resistances, and a couple of defensive layers. If you're dying a lot, check resistances first, then life.
