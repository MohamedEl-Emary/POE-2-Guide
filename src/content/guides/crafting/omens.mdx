An <GlossaryTerm id="omen" /> changes how the **next** currency item you use behaves. On its own an Omen does nothing; it waits in your inventory and applies to the next matching orb.

## What Omens do

Omens give you more control over crafting. Common examples include making an orb affect only prefixes or only suffixes, or making an Exalted Orb add more than one modifier. For example, <Item name="Omen of Sinistral Exaltation" /> makes your next <Item name="Exalted Orb" /> add only a prefix, and <Item name="Omen of Whittling" /> makes your next <Item name="Chaos Orb" /> remove the lowest level modifier. Each Omen describes exactly what it changes, so read it carefully. You can see [every Omen](/items/currency/#omens) on one page.

## Where they come from

Most Omens come from <GlossaryTerm id="ritual" />, where you spend tribute earned at Ritual altars on rewards. The Abyss mechanic is another notable source of certain Omens, and farming them is a popular strategy.

## How to use one

1. Make sure the Omen is in your **main inventory**, not your stash.
2. Use the currency item it affects.
3. The Omen is consumed and the orb behaves differently.

<Callout type="warning">
  An Omen in your inventory triggers on the next matching orb, even if you forgot it was there. Keep Omens in your stash until you mean to use them.
</Callout>

## Should beginners use them?

Omens are mainly for deliberate, higher-value crafting. As a new player, the best thing to do with most Omens is **sell them**. They're often worth a lot to crafters.
